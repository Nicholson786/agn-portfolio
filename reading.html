READING LIST

The books and frameworks outside the published work that shaped the CI philosophy behind it. Not a curriculum — a record of what landed, and why it mattered in context of the work.

// LEAN & CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
[ INFLUENCE 001 ]
2 Second Lean
Paul Akers
FastCap Press
WASTE ELIMINATION AT THE MICRO SCALE

The central premise: find 2 seconds of waste in every task and eliminate it. Not as a project, not as a Kaizen event — as a daily discipline applied everywhere, by everyone. The compound effect of that mindset, sustained across an entire organization, is transformational.

What makes Akers' approach distinctive is its refusal to make lean theoretical. The waste is on the floor, in the workflow, in the motion pattern. You film it, you see it, you fix it, you film the fix. The documentation loop is baked in. The learning compounds. This is "eliminate the conditions" at its most granular — applied not to systems but to seconds.

// CHANGE LEADERSHIP
[ INFLUENCE 002 ]
Leading Change
John P. Kotter
Harvard Business Review Press // 1996
WHY CHANGE FAILS — AND HOW TO MAKE IT HOLD

Kotter's central argument: most organizational change efforts fail not because the plan is wrong, but because the change is never anchored in the culture. An 8-step model for leading transformation — not managing it — through urgency, coalition building, short-term wins, and cultural integration.

The final stage of Kotter's model — "anchor new approaches in the culture" — maps directly onto the Compound stage of the CI maturity model. The hardest part of change is not the change itself. It's making the change the new normal so thoroughly that the old way no longer feels possible. That's the transition from improvement event to improvement culture.
