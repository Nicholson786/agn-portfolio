> ACCESSING ORIGIN RECORD... 2008 — PRESENT

ORIGIN STORY

This isn't a resume. It's a record of how a practitioner gets built — through accumulation, not assignment. Every role created the conditions for the next one. None of it was accidental.

2008
// CHAPTER ONE

THE FLOOR
IS THE LAB.

The first position isn't in an office. It's on the production floor of one of the largest ceramic tile manufacturers in North America. 2008. Dal-Tile. Operator.

Operations at industrial scale teaches things that no curriculum provides: how systems behave under constraint, what the difference between a documented process and a lived one looks like, and exactly how much work it takes to hold production targets when the standard isn't holding. The floor is where the instinct for improvement comes from. It doesn't come from a textbook.

DAL-TILE CORPORATION // MUSKOGEE, OK // 2008
↑
// CHAPTER TWO

EARN THE TEAM.
THEN LEAD IT.

Leadership at the floor level isn't granted. It's recognized. The team notices who they look to when things go wrong. That pattern, repeated under enough pressure, becomes a role.

Rising to lead a production team meant owning outcomes, not just tasks. Cross-functional coordination under time pressure. Accountability that didn't stop at the shift change. The obligation wasn't to the title — it was to the people and to the process. That's the order that matters.

PRODUCTION TEAM LEAD // OPERATIONAL CREDIBILITY ESTABLISHED
OWN
// CHAPTER THREE

TOTAL OWNERSHIP.
ALL OF IT.

The inflection point is a decision, not an assignment. Becoming responsible for a specific piece of industrial equipment — how it runs and why it fails — meant accepting something most operators don't: that the boundary between "operations" and "maintenance" is a human invention, not a mechanical one.

Total ownership means you don't call someone else when it breaks. You understand the machine well enough to run it optimally and to know when it's beginning to fail before it does. That dual perspective — operator-level and technician-level, simultaneously — is what makes the difference between someone who runs a machine and someone who understands a system.

OPERATIONS + MAINTENANCE // ONE OWNER // ZERO HANDOFFS
2019
// CHAPTER FOUR

INTO MAINTENANCE.
ALL THE WAY.

That total-ownership mindset became a career trajectory. If you know a machine well enough to operate it at peak performance and diagnose failure before it happens, you belong in maintenance.

Three years as an industrial maintenance technician at Dal-Tile. Hands on equipment. Diagnosing under time pressure. Repairing correctly the first time. Coordinating spare parts to minimize downtime impact. Working cross-functionally with operations so that quality of repair never gets sacrificed for speed of restart.

What propelled the transition wasn't a vacancy — it was a body of knowledge that said: this is where I belong next. That instinct has been right every time it's been followed.

DAL-TILE // INDUSTRIAL MAINTENANCE TECHNICIAN // 2019–2022
EDU
// CHAPTER FIVE

EDUCATION FOLLOWS
EXPERIENCE.

The degrees didn't come first. They came after a decade of operational reality — and they were better for it.

An Associate in Arts from Connors State College: completed thirteen years into a career already underway, because the credential mattered enough to go back for it. A BS in Industrial Engineering Technology from the University of Southern Mississippi: the degree that named what the floor had already been teaching. An MS in Engineering Management from the University of Oklahoma: turning operational instinct into formal systems framework.

Every credential formalized something that experience had already proven. That sequence — prove it, then name it — is the only one that holds under real operational pressure.

AA // BS INDUSTRIAL ENGINEERING TECHNOLOGY // MS ENGINEERING MANAGEMENT
2022
// CHAPTER SIX

THE PROFESSIONAL
TRANSITION.

Vallourec. A French multinational, world leader in premium tubular solutions for the energy sector. The starting condition: no formal maintenance process.

The technical knowledge accumulated over fourteen years on the floor made this possible: entering as Maintenance Planner, building the maintenance system from zero using SAP CMMS as the operational backbone, leading a thirteen-technician team through the transition from reactive to predictive maintenance, achieving and sustaining a 90% PM completion rate. Then taking on Project Manager and Foreign Trade Zone Administrator responsibilities simultaneously — running compliance with genuine legal consequence at multinational scale.

The professional role didn't come from a classroom. It came from the floor, through the shop, through the maintenance bay, through the systems built from nothing. That's the sequence.

VALLOUREC // MAINTENANCE PLANNER → SUPERVISOR → PM // FTZ ADMIN // 2022–2025
2025
// CHAPTER SEVEN

THEY KNOW WHAT
THIS LOOKS LIKE.
SO DO I.

Returning to Dal-Tile as a Process Engineer closes a loop that seventeen years of compound experience built. The organization knows what operational excellence at this level looks like. So does the person they brought back to design it.

Current work integrates everything: floor-level credibility, maintenance system architecture, project management discipline, foreign trade compliance, published CI frameworks, active doctoral research, and three operational applications built from scratch to address gaps that no off-the-shelf solution was solving.

This is not a career that was planned from the top down. It was built from the floor up — one compound improvement at a time.

DAL-TILE CORPORATION // PROCESS ENGINEER // 2025 — PRESENT
