TECHNICAL FOUNDATION DEEPENED
YOU LEARN A MACHINE BY TAKING IT APART.

Before you can engineer a system, you have to understand it from the inside — not the documentation version, the real one. Three years as an industrial maintenance technician meant diagnosing failures under time pressure, repairing equipment correctly the first time, and knowing the machines well enough to predict where they'd fail next.

This wasn't theoretical. This was hands on the equipment — troubleshooting root causes, coordinating spare parts to minimize downtime, and working directly with operations to keep production moving without sacrificing the repair quality that prevents the next breakdown.

  • Diagnosed and repaired equipment failures across the production floor
  • Built deep mechanical and technical knowledge of the equipment portfolio
  • Coordinated spare parts management to minimize downtime impact
  • Worked cross-functionally with operations to balance uptime with repair integrity
  • Developed the technical foundation that would later inform formal maintenance system design
COMPLEXITY IS JUST ANOTHER SYSTEM
WAITING TO BE MAPPED.

Vallourec is a French multinational — a world leader in premium tubular solutions for the energy industry. Operating at that scale, in that regulatory environment, with that degree of cross-border complexity, is a different category of challenge.

There was no formal maintenance process. That was the starting condition. Entering as Maintenance Planner and moving into Maintenance Supervisor, I built one from the ground up — leading a team of 13 technicians, implementing SAP CMMS as the operational backbone, and driving the transition from reactive to predictive maintenance to a sustained 90% PM completion rate.

Simultaneously led safety culture transformation — researching and deploying new guarding solutions that engineered safer conditions into the environment while shifting the team's mindset to treat safety as an operating priority, not a compliance checkbox.

From there, transitioned rapidly into dual responsibility as Project Manager and Foreign Trade Zone Administrator — running both simultaneously. FTZ administration at a multinational manufacturer carries real financial and legal consequence if it fails. Google Project Management certification formalized the project architecture capability the work had already proven.

  • Built formal maintenance system from zero using SAP CMMS
  • Led 13-technician team through full process transformation
  • Achieved and sustained 90% PM completion rate
  • Researched and deployed new guarding solutions — engineered safety into the environment
  • Promoted through three distinct roles in rapid succession — Planner → Supervisor → PM/FTZ Admin
  • Administered Foreign Trade Zone compliance at multinational manufacturing scale with zero compliance failures
